## Next step

A wake breadcrumb in the M1's own stage-2 boot loader (`/boot/efi/m1n1/boot.bin`, `0a1e47f3`, Nov 16). It publishes `asahi,s2r-mailbox` and has an "RVBAR entry on primary CPU" path, but leaves no mark at a wake. The hypervisor m1n1 (`~/m1n1-next` `bd6a43d4`; a copy sits at `/boot/efi/s2r/m1n1-bd6a43d.bin`) already does this:
- at a wake, the boot CPU enters m1n1 at its locked RVBAR;
- m1n1 reads the always-on block's wake status `+0x1c/+0x20/+0x24` (a macOS wake read `+0x20=0x400000`);
- it jumps to a handler address written into power-chip scratch `0x9f04`.. at the commit, checked by a nonce.

Evidence: `docs/evidence/2026-11-06-mac27-hv/README.md` (breadcrumb from the RVBAR entry), `docs/evidence/2026-11-16-hv-macos135-s2r/README.md` (the wake entry line). The 11-17 evidence already lists this as the way to tell "whether a wake began" ("Not done").

One write inside `0x9f04`-`0x9f0d` at the loader's wake entry, then one ds29 sleep:
- breadcrumb present after the reset: the SoC powered down and woke; the loader's hand-off failed;
- absent: no wake reached the CPU.

This changes the boot loader the M1 starts every day: Naeem's go is needed; keep the current `boot.bin` and a restore script beside it, as with the SIO change (`restore-pre-sio.sh`).

Also from the hypervisor build, for when a wake does arrive: at the wake the P cluster is unpowered, and the secondaries' RVBAR must be restored to their reset page.
